How the plan is put together

The GRE General Test is just under two hours: two Verbal Reasoning sections, two Quantitative Reasoning sections and one Analytical Writing task, “Analyze an Issue”.

  1. Content review, the first half. Quant topics and verbal question types, a couple a day, with practice questions to finish.
  2. Practice, the second half. A full practice test every other week, timed sections in between, and one timed essay a week.
  3. Final week. Your last practice test about five days out, light review, and a day off before the test.

Quant and verbal get about the same total time, with less for the essay. Any area you tick as a worry gets 50% more.

Use the free official practice tests from ETS, and check current rules at ets.org/gre.

What makes a plan stick

A plan that survives week two beats a perfect one that doesn’t.

  • Study at the same time each day. Your phone goes in another room for that block.
  • Keep a log of every miss and why. Re-read it before each study day.
  • When you miss a day, don’t double up. Carry on from where the plan says you are.

Questions people ask

How long should I study for the GRE?

Many people study for 1 to 3 months and put in around 40 to 120 hours, depending on their starting score and target.

How many practice tests should I take?

At least the free official ones, spaced out across the second half of your prep, with a full review of each.

Is this an official GRE plan?

No. It’s a free planning tool from FocusFlow Lab, and it isn’t connected to ETS.
