How the plan is put together

The PANCE tests what new physician assistants need across the organ systems, along with tasks like history-taking, diagnosis and pharmacology. Cardiovascular and pulmonary carry the most questions, so they get the most time here.

  1. Systems review, a bit under half. A few systems a week, each day ending with questions on what you covered.
  2. Mixed practice, the rest. Question blocks across all systems, a timed practice exam every other week, and focused work on your weakest areas.
  3. Final week. Error log and high-yield review, nothing new, and a day off before the exam.

Topic hours follow the rough share of each organ system on the NCCPA content blueprint. Any system you tick as shaky gets 50% more time. These shares are rough, not official.

Check the current blueprint at nccpa.net before you rely on it.

What makes a plan stick

A plan that survives week two beats a perfect one that doesn’t.

  • Study at the same time each day. Your phone goes in another room for that block.
  • Keep a log of every miss and why. Re-read it before each study day.
  • When you miss a day, don’t double up. Carry on from where the plan says you are.

Questions people ask

How long should I study for the PANCE?

Many students give it 4 to 12 weeks after graduation, often around 150 to 350 hours, depending on how their rotations went.

How many practice questions should I do?

Many students work through one full question bank and take a few timed practice exams, reviewing every question.

Is this an official PANCE plan?

No. It’s a free planning tool from FocusFlow Lab, and it isn’t connected to the NCCPA.

Can I save the plan?

Use “Print or save as PDF” and choose Save as PDF. Your answers also stay in this browser for next time.
