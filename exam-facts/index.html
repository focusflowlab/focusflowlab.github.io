NCLEX-RN

  • The NCLEX-RN gives you between 85 and 150 questions, with up to 5 hours to finish.
  • You can retake it up to 8 times a year, at least 45 days apart, unless your jurisdiction sets a stricter limit.

Sources: NCSBN: NCLEX candidate bulletin. Make a schedule with the free NCLEX-RN study plan generator.

NCLEX-PN

  • The NCLEX-PN gives you between 85 and 205 questions, with up to 5 hours to finish.
  • Retakes need at least 45 days between attempts, and some jurisdictions allow fewer attempts a year than the standard limit.

Sources: NCSBN: NCLEX candidate bulletin. Make a schedule with the free NCLEX-PN study plan generator.

ATI TEAS

  • 170 questions in 209 minutes across four sections: Reading 45 questions in 55 minutes, Math 38 in 57 minutes, Science 50 in 60 minutes and English and Language Usage 37 in 37 minutes.
  • Some of those questions are unscored practice items, so not every question counts toward your score.

Sources: ATI: TEAS exam details. Make a schedule with the free ATI TEAS study plan generator.

MCAT

  • 230 questions in four sections: Chem/Phys 59 questions in 95 minutes, CARS 53 in 90 minutes, Bio/Biochem 59 in 95 minutes and Psych/Soc 59 in 95 minutes.
  • About 6 hours 15 minutes of testing, and about 7 hours 30 minutes in the seat with breaks.
  • You can take it up to 3 times in a testing year, 4 times over 2 consecutive years and 7 times in your lifetime.

Sources: AAMC: what is on the MCAT exam, AAMC: testing attempt limits. Make a schedule with the free MCAT study plan generator.

USMLE Step 1

  • Step 1 is one 8-hour test day. Since 14 May 2026 it runs as 30-minute blocks of up to 20 questions each; before that it was 60-minute blocks of up to 40.
  • Step 1 has been reported pass or fail only since 26 January 2022.

Sources: USMLE: Step 1, USMLE: examination results and scoring. Make a schedule with the free USMLE Step 1 study plan generator.

PANCE

  • The current NCCPA blueprint took effect in January 2025. It gives one percentage per area, and says the shares may vary slightly on each exam.

Sources: NCCPA: PANCE blueprint. Make a schedule with the free PANCE study plan generator.

NAPLEX

  • 225 questions in 6 hours, on the computer.
  • Results are reported as pass or fail, with no numeric score.

Sources: NABP: take the NAPLEX. Make a schedule with the free NAPLEX study plan generator.

DAT

  • 280 questions in four tests: Survey of the Natural Sciences 100, Perceptual Ability 90, Reading Comprehension 50 and Quantitative Reasoning 40.
  • There must be at least 60 days between attempts.

Sources: ADA: Dental Admission Test. Make a schedule with the free DAT study plan generator.

GRE

  • About 1 hour 58 minutes in five sections. Analytical Writing comes first: one task in 30 minutes.
  • Verbal Reasoning has two sections (12 questions in 18 minutes, then 15 in 23) and Quantitative Reasoning has two (12 questions in 21 minutes, then 15 in 26).
  • Verbal and Quant adapt at the section level: how you do on the first section sets the difficulty of the second.

Sources: ETS: GRE General Test structure. Make a schedule with the free GRE study plan generator.

GMAT Focus

  • 64 questions in 2 hours 15 minutes across three sections: Quantitative Reasoning, Verbal Reasoning and Data Insights.
  • You choose the section order, and you can review and change up to 3 answers in each section. The total score runs from 205 to 805.

Sources: GMAC: GMAT Focus exam policies. Make a schedule with the free GMAT Focus study plan generator.

LSAT

  • Four sections of 35 minutes each with a 10-minute break after the second. Two Logical Reasoning sections and one Reading Comprehension section are scored, and the fourth is unscored.
  • Argumentative Writing is separate: 50 minutes in total, with 15 minutes of prewriting and 35 minutes of writing.
  • LSAC has announced the LSAT will change from August 2027, so check the current format before you test.

Sources: LSAC: LSAT FAQs, LSAC: the 2027 LSAT change. Make a schedule with the free LSAT study plan generator.

CPA FAR

  • Each section is 4 hours with two multiple-choice testlets and three task-based simulation testlets. It is scored 0 to 99, you need 75 to pass, and the score is not a percentage or a curve; multiple-choice and simulations count equally. FAR has 50 multiple-choice questions and 7 simulations.
  • FAR content areas: Financial Reporting 30 to 40%, Select Balance Sheet Accounts 30 to 40%, Select Transactions 25 to 35%.

Sources: AICPA: what is tested on the CPA Exam, AICPA: CPA Exam scoring, ThisWayToCPA: what every candidate should know about FAR. Make a schedule with the free CPA FAR study plan generator.

CPA AUD

  • Each section is 4 hours with two multiple-choice testlets and three task-based simulation testlets. It is scored 0 to 99, you need 75 to pass, and the score is not a percentage or a curve; multiple-choice and simulations count equally. AUD has 78 multiple-choice questions and 7 simulations.

Sources: AICPA: what is tested on the CPA Exam, AICPA: CPA Exam scoring. Make a schedule with the free CPA AUD study plan generator.

CPA REG

  • Each section is 4 hours with two multiple-choice testlets and three task-based simulation testlets. It is scored 0 to 99, you need 75 to pass, and the score is not a percentage or a curve; multiple-choice and simulations count equally. REG has 72 multiple-choice questions and 8 simulations.

Sources: AICPA: what is tested on the CPA Exam, AICPA: CPA Exam scoring. Make a schedule with the free CPA REG study plan generator.
