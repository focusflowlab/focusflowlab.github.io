    How to use it

    After each practice set, add how many you got right and the topic. The table shows your accuracy per topic and where your next week’s hours should go.

    • Log the topic, not just the total. A 70% overall can hide one topic at 40%.
    • Small sets are noisy. Under about 20 questions in a topic, treat the number as a hint, not a verdict.
    • Prefer a spreadsheet? Download the practice score log (Excel or Google Sheets, with a % right chart).
    • Redo what you missed. Pair this with the error log so each miss gets a retest date.
    • Feed it back into your plan. Use the suggested split to set next week’s hours in the free exam study plan.

    Questions people ask

    Where are my scores stored?

    In this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere. Use “Download as CSV” to keep a copy or move to another device.

    How is the suggested share worked out?

    Each topic starts from its rough weight in the exam outline. A topic below your target accuracy gets a larger share, in proportion to how far below it is. A topic with no data gets a small boost until you test it. Then the shares are scaled to add up to 100%.

    Are the topic weights official?

    No. They are the rough shares the free plan generators use, based on each exam’s published outline. Check the official outline for the current version.

    What target should I use?

    Use your own practice-test goal. If you are unsure, 70 to 80% is a common planning target, but it is not an official passing mark for any exam.

    Which exams does it cover?

    NCLEX-RN, NCLEX-PN, ATI TEAS, MCAT, USMLE Step 1, PANCE, NAPLEX, DAT, GRE, GMAT Focus, LSAT and the three CPA sections.
