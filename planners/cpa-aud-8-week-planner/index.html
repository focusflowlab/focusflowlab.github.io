About the CPA Exam AUD section

AUD (Auditing and Attestation) is one of the three core CPA sections. It tests the audit process from planning to the report, plus ethics and independence, with multiple-choice questions and task-based simulations.

This 8-week planner turns that into a calendar you can write on: the weekly overview shows the whole plan, and the daily pages split each study day into four focus blocks that end with a self-test.

The exam at a glance

  • Each section is 4 hours with two multiple-choice testlets and three task-based simulation testlets. It is scored 0 to 99, you need 75 to pass, and the score is not a percentage or a curve; multiple-choice and simulations count equally. AUD has 78 multiple-choice questions and 7 simulations.

Checked against the official pages in November 2026; the exam owner can change these. Sources: AICPA: what is tested on the CPA Exam, AICPA: CPA Exam scoring.

How to use the 8 weeks

  • Weeks 1 to 4: content review. Take one or two topics a day and finish each day with practice questions on what you covered.
  • Weeks 5 to 8: practice and review. Move to timed sets and full practice tests, write every miss in the error log, and keep going back to your weakest areas.
  • The last days. Work through the test-day checklist and keep the final day light.

Most people planning for the CPA Exam AUD section put in about 90 to 150 hours in total, which is roughly 11 to 19 hours a week over 8 weeks. That is a planning range, not an official figure: start from your own score and target.

How many hours should I study for AUD?

Many candidates plan around 90 to 150 hours for AUD, spread over 6 to 10 weeks while working.

Is AUD easier than FAR?

Most candidates find AUD has less material than FAR, but it’s more about judgement and wording, so practise reading questions carefully.
