About USMLE Step 1

Step 1 is a one-day, roughly eight-hour exam and is now scored pass or fail. Questions mix basic science with the organ systems, so the schedule is built around systems, with general principles (biochemistry, genetics, immunology, microbiology and pharmacology) as its own block.

This 8-week planner turns that into a calendar you can write on: the weekly overview shows the whole plan, and the daily pages split each study day into four focus blocks that end with a self-test.

The exam at a glance

  • Step 1 is one 8-hour test day. Since 14 May 2026 it runs as 30-minute blocks of up to 20 questions each; before that it was 60-minute blocks of up to 40.
  • Step 1 has been reported pass or fail only since 26 January 2022.

Checked against the official pages in November 2026; the exam owner can change these. Sources: USMLE: Step 1, USMLE: examination results and scoring.

How to use the 8 weeks

  • Weeks 1 to 4: content review. Take one or two topics a day and finish each day with practice questions on what you covered.
  • Weeks 5 to 8: practice and review. Move to timed sets and full practice tests, write every miss in the error log, and keep going back to your weakest areas.
  • The last days. Work through the test-day checklist and keep the final day light.

Most people planning for USMLE Step 1 put in about 250 to 500 hours in total, which is roughly 31 to 62 hours a week over 8 weeks. That is a planning range, not an official figure: start from your own score and target.

How long is a Step 1 dedicated period?

Many students take 4 to 8 weeks of dedicated study at 8 to 10 hours a day, after building a base during the preclinical years.

How many practice questions should I do?

Many students finish one full question bank, roughly 3,000 to 4,000 questions, and review each block the same day.
