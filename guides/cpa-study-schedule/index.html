CPA study schedule: how to plan all four sections

Four sections, one credit window. Here’s how to order them and how long to give each.

By FocusFlow Lab. Published 28 October 2026.

On this page

  1. How the CPA Exam is structured
  2. Which order to take them in
  3. How long each section takes
  4. A full timeline
  5. Plan each section week by week

How the CPA Exam is structured

Since January 2024, the CPA Exam has three core sections that everyone takes, plus one discipline section you choose:

  • Core: Financial Accounting and Reporting (FAR), Auditing and Attestation (AUD), and Taxation and Regulation (REG).
  • Discipline (pick one): Business Analysis and Reporting (BAR), Information Systems and Controls (ISC), or Tax Compliance and Planning (TCP).

Each section is a 4-hour exam, and you need a score of 75 to pass. You take them one at a time, in any order, and must pass all four within your state’s credit window. That window used to be 18 months in every state; many states now allow 30 months. Check your own board of accountancy’s rules before you plan.

Which order to take them in

There’s no required order, but these approaches work well for most candidates:

  • FAR first if you have the most time now. It’s usually the longest section, and it gives you a base for the others.
  • Pair related sections. REG and TCP overlap on tax, and FAR and BAR overlap on financial reporting, so taking them back to back saves review time.
  • Put your busiest work season on your shortest section, or leave a gap. Don’t start FAR in the middle of busy season.

A full timeline

Studying part time while working, most candidates finish all four sections in about 12 to 18 months:

  1. Months 1 to 3: FAR. Book your exam date before you start, so the plan has an end.
  2. Months 4 to 6: AUD or REG.
  3. Months 7 to 9: the other core section.
  4. Months 10 to 12: your discipline section, paired with the core section it overlaps most.

Leave a week or two between sections to rest, and keep a buffer before your credit window closes in case you need to retake one.
