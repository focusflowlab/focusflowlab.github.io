Exam length and time per question: 12 exams compared

PANCE gives you about 1 minute per question. The GMAT Focus Edition gives about 2.1. The NCLEX allows 2 to 3.5 minutes, depending on how many questions you get. The table has the rest.

By FocusFlow Lab. Published 25 November 2026.

On this page

  1. The short answer
  2. Exam length compared
  3. What the numbers mean for pacing
  4. Build a plan for your exam

The short answer

The fastest pace in this table is the PANCE, at about 1 minute per question (300 questions in 300 minutes). The slowest is the NCLEX, which allows up to 5 hours for 85 to 150 questions. The other exams sit between 1.2 and 2.1 minutes per question. The question and time figures are the exam owners' published numbers. Minutes per question and questions per hour are our own arithmetic.

Exam length compared

Where an exam has several sections, we added the section times and counted only the questions each owner lists. The CPA sections mix multiple-choice questions with task-based simulations that take much longer than a multiple-choice question, so a single minutes-per-question figure would mislead and we leave it out.

The figures are rounded. The NCLEX range comes from dividing 300 minutes by 150 and by 85 questions. The GRE row counts the Verbal and Quantitative Reasoning sections only (12 + 15 + 12 + 15 questions in 18 + 23 + 21 + 26 minutes). The MCAT row divides 375 testing minutes by 230 questions. Not every owner lists its break time, so a missing break note here means we have not checked it, not that there are no breaks. USMLE Step 1 and the DAT are not in the table because we have not checked both a question count and a total time for them; see exam formats at a glance for what we have.

What the numbers mean for pacing

  • Under 1.5 minutes per question (PANCE, TEAS): speed is the skill. Practise in timed blocks, not open-ended sets.
  • Around 1.6 minutes (MCAT, NAPLEX, GRE): you can read a stem and think, but not for long. Long timed sets matter.
  • 2 minutes or more (GMAT, NCLEX): harder, longer questions, so stamina and decision making count more than raw speed.
