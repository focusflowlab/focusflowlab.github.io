How to study for an exam while working full time

You will not find ten free hours a day. You can find a few small, protected blocks, and plan the exam around them.

By FocusFlow Lab. Published 18 November 2026.

On this page

  1. Count the hours you really have
  2. Use short blocks every day
  3. Build a plan that survives a bad week
  4. Protect the habit

Count the hours you really have

Work out your weekly study hours honestly: weekday evenings, mornings, lunch breaks, weekend blocks. Then multiply by the weeks to the exam. The exam countdown shows how many study hours that is. If the number is short for your exam, the fix is a later date, not a heroic plan you will drop in week two.

Use short blocks every day

  • Aim for 60 to 90 minutes on workdays and one longer block at the weekend.
  • Keep the same time each day so it becomes a habit rather than a decision.
  • Use the weak spots in the day for recall and flashcards, and the strong spots for new material.

Build a plan that survives a bad week

Enter your real hours into the exam study plan generator, not the hours you wish you had. Keep one catch-up block a week. If a week is lost to overtime, move the sessions forward instead of trying to cram them into the next one.

Protect the habit

  • Do the first block before the day gets away from you.
  • Turn off distractions. See how to stop checking your phone while studying.
  • Review what you got wrong each weekend with the error log.
