How to stop checking your phone while studying

Willpower loses to a phone within reach. Distance and friction win.

By FocusFlow Lab. Published 26 October 2026.

On this page

  1. Why willpower keeps losing
  2. Put distance between you and it
  3. Add friction to every check
  4. Give your brain a time to check
  5. Write down what pulls you away
  6. Watch the video

Why willpower keeps losing

Every time your phone lights up, part of your attention goes with it, even if you don’t pick it up. Researchers at the University of Texas found that people did worse on attention and memory tasks when their phone was simply on the desk, face down and silent, than when it was in another room (Ward et al., 2017).

So the goal isn’t to resist the phone better. It’s to make checking it slow and awkward enough that you don’t bother.

Put distance between you and it

The simplest barrier is space. Before a focus block, put the phone in another room, or at least in a bag across the room. If you need it for two-factor codes or emergencies, leave it where you can hear a call but can’t see the screen.

Face down on the desk doesn’t count. It’s still in reach, and still pulling at you.

Add friction to every check

Make each check take a few extra steps:

  • Put the phone in a drawer, a zipped bag or a box with a lid.
  • Turn on Focus mode or Do Not Disturb, and allow only calls from a few people.
  • Log out of the one app you open without thinking, for the length of the block.

Each step is small. Together they turn “just checking” into a decision, and most of the time you’ll decide not to.

Give your brain a time to check

Checking is easier to skip when you know when you can do it. Study in set blocks, then check your phone in the break. The focus timer does this for you: a 25 to 50 minute block, then a short break.

Write down what pulls you away

Keep a scrap of paper next to you. When you feel the urge to check, write down why: a message you’re waiting for, boredom, a hard question. After a few days you’ll see a pattern, and you can plan for it. The free Study Focus Kit has a worksheet for exactly this.
