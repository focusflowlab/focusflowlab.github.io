A USMLE Step 1 study schedule for your dedicated period

Step 1 is pass/fail, so the aim is to cover everything and pass with room to spare. Here is an 8-week way to do it.

By FocusFlow Lab. Published 2 November 2026.

On this page

  1. How Step 1 works now
  2. Plan for passing, not for a score
  3. Weeks 1 to 5: systems, one at a time
  4. Weeks 6 and 7: mixed blocks and self-assessment
  5. Week 8: taper
  6. How many hours?
  7. Make it fit your dates

How Step 1 works now

Step 1 results have been reported as pass or fail only since 26 January 2022, so there is no three-digit score to chase. It is taken in a single 8-hour session.

The question format changed in 2026. For exams on or after 14 May 2026, the USMLE lists fourteen 30-minute blocks of up to 20 questions each, with at least 55 minutes of break time. Before that date it was seven 60-minute blocks of up to 40 questions. Check usmle.org for the format on your test date, because it can change again.

Plan for passing, not for a score

With a pass/fail result, the useful question is “where am I weakest?”, not “how high can I go?”. Aim to touch every system at least twice and to spend extra time where your practice results are lowest.

Take one self-assessment early. It shows where to start and gives you a baseline to compare later ones against.

Weeks 1 to 5: systems, one at a time

  • Study one organ system at a time, such as cardiovascular, respiratory, renal and so on, with the basic science behind it: physiology, pathology and pharmacology together.
  • Do a timed block of practice questions every day, then review every question, right or wrong, before moving on.
  • Keep an error log with one line per miss: a content gap, a misread, or a timing problem.
  • Review flashcards for drugs, pathways and findings a little every day. The free flashcards space the reviews for you.

Weeks 6 and 7: mixed blocks and self-assessment

  • Switch to mixed blocks that cover several systems at once, because the real exam does not announce the topic.
  • Take a full-length self-assessment under test conditions, at the time of day your exam is booked.
  • Spend as long reviewing it as taking it, and send what you learn back into the systems you are weakest in.

Week 8: taper

One more self-assessment early in the week, then your error log and short question sets. Keep the last two days light, check your test centre route and ID, and sleep. A full-length exam the day before only adds fatigue.

How many hours?

Many students use 4 to 8 weeks of dedicated study, with 8 to 10 hours a day on study days. That is a common planning range, not an official figure, and it depends on how recently you finished the basic science courses. If you are short on time, cut the weeks and keep the daily question blocks.
