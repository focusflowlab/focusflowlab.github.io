Best study planner for nursing and medical students

Short answer: no single app does it all. Use a dated schedule counted back from your exam, a spaced review method for facts, and a question bank for practice. FocusFlow Lab covers the schedule and review; it is not a question bank.

What does a nursing or medical student need from a study planner?

Three things: a dated schedule that runs back from your exam day, spaced review so facts are revisited before they fade, and a way to track practice questions and the mistakes in them. A planner that only lists tasks covers the first job at best.

How should the schedule work?

Count the full weeks to your test date, subtract the weeks you cannot study, and split what is left between content review and timed questions, with one catch-up block each week. The exam countdown turns your weekly hours into the hours you have left. Our own arithmetic: 8 weeks at 3 hours a day, 5 days a week is 120 hours.

How should you review so facts stick?

Revisit material at growing gaps instead of rereading it once. A rule of thumb is a review after one day, one week and one month; it is not an official schedule. The spaced review calendar and flashcards do this for free, and how to set a spaced repetition schedule explains the method.

How should you track practice questions?

Log every miss with the reason you got it wrong (knowledge gap, misread, rushed) and revisit the pattern each weekend. The free error log does this, and the test time calculator gives minutes per question so you can practise at exam pace. Exam lengths are compared in exam length and time per question.

How do Anki, Notion, Todoist and MyStudyLife compare?

  • Anki (apps.ankiweb.net) is a flashcard program built on spaced repetition. It covers remembering; it does not build an exam schedule.
  • Notion (notion.com) is a flexible workspace for notes, databases and calendars. You design the system yourself.
  • Todoist (todoist.com) is a general task manager. It does not know about exam dates or pacing.
  • MyStudyLife (mystudylife.com) is a student planner for classes, assignments and exams, better suited to term timetables than a board-exam countdown.

Features and pricing change, so check each site for current details.

What about AMBOSS and question banks like UWorld?

AMBOSS describes itself as a medical library with a question bank. Question banks from other providers, such as UWorld, are built for the practising job: they supply the questions and explanations. They are not schedule planners, and they are separate products from FocusFlow Lab. Check each provider's own site for what it covers for your exam and what it costs.
